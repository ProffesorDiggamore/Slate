Privacy

Last updated November 5, 2026

The short version: Slate has no server, no accounts and no analytics. Your notes are stored in your browser on your own device and, if you turn on sync, in a GitHub repository that you own. Whoever runs this website can't see your notes, your calendar or your passwords — the site only sends the app's code to your browser. The AI assistant is optional and off until you add your own key; then your questions go straight from your device to the AI service you picked, and only when you ask.

What's kept on your device

  • Your notes, trash and settings, in your browser's storage for this site.
  • Your GitHub access token, if you turn on sync.
  • A Google access token, if you connect Google Calendar. It expires on its own within an hour.
  • Your AI key, if you set up the AI assistant — in the iOS Keychain in the iPhone / iPad app, in your browser's storage for this site on the web. It's never added to your settings, never synced to GitHub and never sent anywhere except to that AI service.
  • Your AI chats, if you use the assistant. They stay on this device and aren't synced.

Each browser keeps its own copy. Someone else using Slate on their own device has a completely separate, private copy.

Where your data goes

  • GitHub (api.github.com) — only if you turn on sync, only the repository you choose, using the token you paste in.
  • Google Calendar (www.googleapis.com) — only if you connect it. See below.
  • The AI service you choose — only if you set up the AI assistant with your own key, and only when you ask it something. See below.
  • Nowhere else. The website tells your browser to block every other destination, so this isn't just a promise. (The App Store app has no list of AI services, since you can point it at any one you like, but it still only talks to the one you set up.)

Links and images you add to your own notes load from wherever they point, like in any editor. The website host (Netlify) keeps the ordinary request logs every web host keeps, such as IP addresses; Slate adds no tracking of its own.

Google Calendar

If you choose to connect Google Calendar, Slate asks Google for permission to:

  • see the list of your calendars, so you can choose which ones to show;
  • see your events, so they appear in Slate's calendar next to your daily notes;
  • add, change and delete events, but only when you tell it to.

Your calendar data goes straight from Google to your browser. It is never sent to, or stored on, any server run by Slate, never shared or sold, never used for advertising, never used to train AI models, and never read by people. Events are kept in memory only while the app is open.

Slate's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

AI assistant

The AI assistant is optional and off until you set it up in Settings → AI. Slate doesn't run an AI service or sell access to one: you bring your own key from the service you choose — ChatGPT (OpenAI), Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google), OpenRouter, Groq, Mistral, DeepSeek, xAI, or a model running on your own computer such as Ollama or LM Studio.

  • Only when you ask. Nothing is sent in the background. When you send a message or use an AI action on selected text, Slate sends that message plus whatever you chose to include — the page you attached, the text you selected, or short excerpts of matching notes if you turned on “Search my notes”.
  • Straight to the service. The request goes directly from your device to that service using your key. It never passes through a server run by Slate, and nobody running Slate can see it.
  • You're asked first. Before the first message to each service, Slate asks for your permission and names the service. You can take that back in Settings → AI.
  • Encrypted pages are never sent, even when they're attached or match a search.
  • The service's own policy applies. What happens to your messages there — how long they're kept, and whether they're used for training — is up to that service and your account with it. Check its privacy policy and settings.

Removing your data

  • From a device: Settings → Data → Reset this device.
  • AI: Settings → AI → Remove key deletes your key from this device. You can also delete the key itself on the AI service's website, which stops it working everywhere. In the iPhone / iPad app, deleting the app doesn't remove the key straight away (iOS keeps Keychain items after an app is deleted); Slate clears it if it's installed again. To remove it now, use Remove key or Reset this device before deleting the app.
  • Google: Settings → Calendar & time → Disconnect, which also cancels Slate's access. You can check at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • GitHub: delete the token at github.com/settings/personal-access-tokens. Your notes repo is yours to keep or delete.

Open source

Slate's code is public at github.com/ProffesorDiggamore/Slate, so anyone can check what it does, or host their own copy. Questions go in that repo's Issues.
